realise it would actually sell — an entry for the
[make.fun bounty](https://make.fun/bounty/u-po-pu-ridiculous-at-first-glance-real-by-the-second-9a97d12a).

## The pitch

> We sell naming rights to potholes: furious drivers pay $49 to name the
> crater that ate their tyre, and the city uses the fee to finally fill it.

Naming rights for a pothole sounds like a joke — until you see the loop:
the driver who suffered pays, the city gets the repair funded, and the
pothole that caused the complaint pays for its own disappearance.

## Why this line

Three pitches were weighed pairwise against each other and against the
obvious absurd ideas (pet rocks, air in a can, subscription socks):

1. **Pothole naming rights (chosen)** — the strangest of the three and the
   only one whose money loop closes inside the sentence: the payer, the
   price, and the repair are all named.
2. A custom doorbell rebuilt from the creak of your childhood front door,
   sold to homesick expats — vivid, but a familiar business (custom sounds)
   with a strange adjective, and a one-off novelty purchase.
3. Breakup texts written in Shakespearean verse at $19 a heartbreak —
   a clear payer, but ghostwriting with a literary adjective bolted on.

Full reasoning, including the pairwise calls, is in [REPORT.md](REPORT.md).
